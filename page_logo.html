Nike Swoosh

1971 - Present Carolyn Davidson

The Nike Swoosh is one of the most recognizable brand symbols in the world, representing motion and speed. Designed by Carolyn Davidson in 1971 for just $35, the logo has become synonymous with athletic excellence and innovation.

Logo Overview

The Nike Swoosh logo is a fluid, dynamic checkmark shape that suggests motion and speed. Its simple yet powerful design has remained largely unchanged since its creation, becoming one of the most successful brand marks in history.

Design Elements

  • Shape: The Swoosh represents the wing of Nike, the Greek goddess of victory
  • Flow: The curved shape implies motion and speed
  • Negative Space: The empty space within the curve creates visual interest
  • Typography: Originally paired with a custom Futura Bold variant (1971-1995)

Cultural Impact

The Swoosh has transcended its role as a corporate logo to become a cultural icon. It appears on everything from sneakers to athlete endorsements, representing not just a brand but an entire lifestyle of athleticism and determination.

DESIGNER

Carolyn Davidson

Graphic Design Student at Portland State University

Davidson was paid $35 for her design in 1971. In 1983, Nike founder Phil Knight gave her a diamond Swoosh ring and an envelope containing Nike stock.

BRAND NAME

Nike is named after the Greek goddess of victory. The company was originally called Blue Ribbon Sports when founded in 1964, changing to Nike, Inc. in 1971 when the Swoosh was introduced.

Historical Evolution

1964-1971: Pre-Swoosh Era

Founded as Blue Ribbon Sports, the company used a simple wordmark with no distinctive logo during its early years distributing Onitsuka Tiger shoes.

1971: Creation of the Swoosh

Carolyn Davidson, a graphic design student at Portland State University, created the Swoosh logo while Phil Knight was teaching accounting classes there. The logo debuted on shoes in June 1971.

1978: Swoosh Standalone

Nike began using the Swoosh without the wordmark in some applications, establishing it as a standalone brand symbol.

1985: Air Jordan Impact

The Air Jordan line helped elevate the Swoosh to cultural icon status, with the logo appearing prominently on Michael Jordan's revolutionary sneakers.

1995: Wordmark Redesign

Nike updated its wordmark to a more contemporary sans-serif typeface while keeping the Swoosh unchanged.

Present: Global Recognition

Today the Swoosh stands alone in most applications, recognized worldwide without needing the Nike name. It remains one of the most valuable brand symbols.

Usage Guidelines

Nike has strict guidelines for logo usage to maintain brand integrity. These are general principles based on publicly available information.

Clear Space

The Nike Swoosh should always have clear space around it equal to at least half the height of the Swoosh. This ensures visibility and prevents visual clutter.

Minimum Size

The Swoosh should never appear smaller than 8mm in height for print applications or 24px in digital applications.

Color Usage

The Swoosh is most commonly displayed in black or white, but can appear in any single color that provides sufficient contrast with the background.

Prohibited Uses

  • Do not alter the proportions of the Swoosh
  • Do not rotate the Swoosh
  • Do not add effects like shadows or gradients
  • Do not combine with other logos or marks
  • Do not use as a pattern or decorative element

Important: These guidelines are for reference only. Actual Nike brand guidelines are proprietary. For official usage rights, contact Nike's legal department.
